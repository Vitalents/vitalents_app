## Verbatim excerpts (NASA, US government work)

- [p. 1, Executive Summary] "Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS) refers to a constellation of ocular findings"
- [p. 3, Optic Disc Edema (ODE)] "70% of crew are reported to have one or more signs of SANS"
- [p. 4, Pathophysiology: Chorioretinal folds] "Choroidal folds are present in approximately 25% of long-duration crewmembers."
- [p. 5, Pathophysiology: Hyperopic vision shifts] "29% of short duration crewmembers and 60% of long duration crew have reported vision degradation."
- [p. 6, Monitoring: Equipment/Method table] "Fundoscopy assesses the gross structural changes in the optic nerve head and retina; detects ODE, CF"
- [p. 8, Monitoring: Equipment Training, In-flight] "Additional testing can be performed as clinically indicated at any point during mission"

## Neutral summary (written by the library agent from this source; not a quotation; needs review)

The technical brief describes SANS as a set of eye findings seen in crew members on long-duration missions, such as swelling of the optic nerve head, folds in the back of the eye and shifts in vision. The leading hypothesis it gives involves the headward fluid shift in microgravity. Monitoring combines preflight, in-flight and postflight eye imaging and vision testing.
