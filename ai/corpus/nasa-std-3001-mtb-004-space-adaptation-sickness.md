- [p. 1, Executive Summary] "is a well-known problem that affects up to 73% of crewmembers during the first 2-3 days of spaceflight."
- [p. 2, Physiology (Space Adaptation Sickness)] "Space Adaptation Sickness (SAS) is a condition experienced by 70-90% of crewmembers during spaceflight,"
- [p. 2, Physiology (Space Adaptation Sickness)] "Initial symptoms occur in most crew in the first 3-7 days."
- [p. 2, Physiology (Space Adaptation Sickness)] "SAS symptoms include congestion, facial fullness, headache, back pain, drowsiness, constipation, urinary retention,"
- [p. 3, Symptoms and Medical Issues Associated with SAS] "Headaches are reported by up to 90% of astronauts during the first few days."
- [p. 5, Physiology (Space Motion Sickness)] "Private Medical Conferences on the ISS are conducted frequently in the first week of spaceflight"
- [p. 8, Non-Pharmacological Countermeasures and Treatments] "Training to minimize head and provocative movements"

## Neutral summary (written by the library agent from this source; not a quotation; needs review)

The medical technical brief describes space adaptation sickness as a set of symptoms many crew members report in the first days of spaceflight, including headache, congestion and facial fullness, as the body adjusts to weightlessness. The brief gives different prevalence figures in different sections. Non-drug measures it lists include training to limit provocative head movements, and private medical conferences are held often in the first week.
