# NASA-STD-3001 Technical Brief: Bone Loss

Publisher: NASA Office of the Chief Health and Medical Officer (OCHMO)
Source: https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/ochmo-tb-030-bone-loss.pdf
Version: OCHMO-MTB-013 Rev D
Published: 2026-02-11. Retrieved: 2026-11-14.
Library domain: Movement and exercise

## Verbatim excerpts

Every line below is quoted from the source as published (NASA work). Locators give the page or section.

> The spaceflight environment of microgravity reduces the loading on muscles and bones.
(p. 2, Physiology: Spaceflight effects on bone health)

> Load bearing exercise stimulates osteoblasts, leading to bone growth and bone density increase.
(p. 12, Reference Data: Countermeasures)

> NASA monitors crew terrestrially pre- and post-flight but no system to date measures bone density in-flight.
(p. 19, Application: BMD loss monitoring)

> Bone density loss in microgravity has been a well documented crew health concern since the Skylab missions
(p. 1, Executive Summary (fragment))

> ISS currently uses the Advanced Resistive Exercise Device (ARED) for resistance and loading exercises.
(p. 12, Reference Data: Countermeasures)

> Microfractures may propagate and result in stress fractures.
(p. 20, Application: Post-flight Monitoring and Testing)
